## 3. Responsibilities when distributing iTune or a modified version

If you distribute or otherwise convey a GPL-covered copy, you must follow the applicable requirements in the GPL. Depending on the form of distribution, these include:

1. **Provide the license.** Include a copy of the applicable GNU GPL license text with the covered work.
2. **Preserve notices.** Keep relevant copyright, license, and warranty-disclaimer notices intact.
3. **Identify your changes.** Modified files must carry appropriate notices stating that you changed them and giving relevant dates, as required by the GPL.
4. **License the covered work correctly.** A covered modified work must be licensed as required by the GPL; you may not impose further restrictions on recipients' exercise of GPL rights.
5. **Provide Corresponding Source.** When distributing object code or an APK, provide the Corresponding Source by one of the methods allowed by the GPL. The source must be the preferred form for making modifications and include the material required by the GPL, such as relevant build and installation scripts.
6. **Provide required installation information.** Where the GPL's User Product provisions apply, comply with the installation-information requirements in section 6 of the GPL.
7. **Respect third-party terms.** Check the separate licenses and notices for libraries, assets, fonts, icons, and other material included with the app.

A link to a repository is useful, but it is not automatically sufficient in every situation. The source offer and delivery method must satisfy the GPL's requirements for the particular way the binary is conveyed. Keep the source corresponding to each distributed release available and clearly identify the matching version or commit.

## 4. Modified versions and forks

You may fork iTune, change its interface, add features, remove features, fix bugs, or adapt it for another purpose.

If you distribute a modified version that is a work based on GPL-covered iTune code, you must comply with the GPL for the covered work. In particular:

- Keep the applicable license and copyright notices.
- Mark your modifications appropriately.
- License the covered modified work under the GPL terms.
- Make the Corresponding Source available as required.
- Do not add legal or technical restrictions that prevent recipients from exercising GPL rights.

You may identify your own changes and your own copyright in them. The GPL does not transfer your copyright in original contributions to the iTune project. It grants permissions to recipients under its terms.

Whether a separate module, plugin, service, or other component forms part of a GPL-covered combined or derivative work depends on the facts and applicable law. Merely placing independent works next to one another on the same storage medium does not automatically make every work subject to the GPL. Do not assume that every component in an app bundle has the same license.

Commercial use is allowed by the GPL. For example, a person or company may:

- Sell copies of a GPL-covered iTune build.
- Charge for installation, configuration, customization, or technical support.
- Include the software in a commercial product, subject to the GPL and all other applicable licenses.
- Offer paid distribution or maintenance.

A distributor must still comply with the GPL when conveying covered copies. Charging money does not remove the obligation to provide the required license notices and Corresponding Source. The GPL does not require a distributor to provide support, updates, or warranty service unless separately agreed.

## 6. Copyright and ownership

Copyright in a contribution generally remains with its author or other lawful rightsholder unless a separate written agreement says otherwise. The GPL is a license: it grants permissions; it does not by itself transfer ownership of copyright to the project maintainer.

The project-level licensing statement applies only to material for which the relevant rightsholders have authorized distribution under those terms. A file or asset with its own copyright or license notice may be governed by different terms. Do not remove or overwrite those notices.

If you contribute code, documentation, or other material, submit only material that you created or are legally authorized to contribute. The contribution guidance in [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) describes the project's intended contribution terms.

## 11. Compliance checklist for redistributors

Before publishing an APK, fork, or other distributed build, check the following:

- [ ] Identify which files and components are actually GPL-covered.
- [ ] Include the correct GPL license text and preserve copyright notices.
- [ ] Clearly identify your modifications and the version you are distributing.
- [ ] Prepare and provide the Corresponding Source using a GPL-compliant method.
- [ ] Include relevant build scripts and other source materials required by the GPL.
- [ ] Provide installation information when required by GPL section 6.
- [ ] Include all required third-party license and attribution notices.
- [ ] Check that bundled assets and dependencies may be redistributed as intended.
- [ ] Do not add extra restrictions to recipients' GPL rights.
- [ ] Avoid implying that your fork is an official or endorsed iTune release.

This checklist is a practical reminder, not a substitute for reading the complete GPL and reviewing the exact distribution method.
